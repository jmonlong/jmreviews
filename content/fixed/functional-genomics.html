Scores for non-coding regions

Vitsios et al. (2021) introduced the JARVIS and gwRVIS scores integrating variation in humans to help predict functional regions across the whole genome. gwRVIS only includes human variation information, while JARVIS integrates other information like genomic sequence and functional annotations. The score is built from the TOPMed data of 60K+ whole-genome sequencing samples. The gwRVIS score is computed with a sliding window approach (size 3kbp, step 1bp) and regression model to predict the number of common variants (MAF >0.1%) in a window based on the total number of variants. They provide a score for each nucleotide (the center of the window). The gwRVIS scores correlates with RVIS and conservation or gene annotation tracks, predicts ClinVar status for non-coding variants better than others. JARVIS, a more advanced machine learning approach to predict pathogenicity integrates gwRVIS, the sequence, context info (e.g. GC content) and functional annotations (epigenetic marks). It was trained on “positive” non-coding ClinVar Pathogenic/Likely Pathogenic variants and “negative” benign variants from the denovo-db database. To annotate SVs, they computed the median, mean, 1st/3rd quartiles of the scores in the affected region, then took the average of those summary statistics to get one score per SV. To compare the performance of the SV scoring, they evaluate the power to predict intergenic SVs from different types of coding/promoter SVs in the gnomAD-SV dataset.
